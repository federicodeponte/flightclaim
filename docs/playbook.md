# DIY claim playbook

> **Not legal advice.** Verify the governing law, deadline, competent body, and
> effect of any parallel proceeding before filing.

## 1. Classify the disruption before calculating

Start with what happened to the booked flight:

- **Cancellation:** the planned flight did not operate. Article 5 controls fixed
  compensation and its notice and re-routing exemptions.
- **Denied boarding:** the flight operated or was intended to operate, but the
  passenger was refused carriage despite meeting Article 3(2), without a
  reasonable ground. Article 4(3) controls.
- **Delay:** the flight operated late. Sturgeon provides Article 7 compensation
  at three hours or more at the final destination, subject to extraordinary
  circumstances.
- **Missed connection:** for directly connecting flights on one reservation,
  Folkerts focuses the compensation assessment on delay at the final
  destination.
- **Downgrade:** Article 10 reimburses a percentage of the fare attributable to
  the downgraded flight.

Do not import Article 5 cancellation exemptions into an Article 4 denied-
boarding claim.

## 2. Verify scope and the responsible carrier

Article 3 covers:

- departures from an airport in an EU Member State, regardless of carrier; and
- arrivals in an EU Member State from a third country when the operating carrier
  is a Community carrier, subject to the third-country benefits proviso.

Use the operating carrier, defined in Article 2(b), rather than assuming that the
seller or marketing carrier is responsible.

## 3. Run the timing tests literally

For cancellation notice given less than seven days before departure,
Article 5(1)(c)(iii) exempts Article 7 compensation only when both are true:

1. the offered re-routing departs no more than one hour before the passenger's
   own originally scheduled departure; and
2. it reaches the final destination less than two hours after the passenger's
   own originally scheduled arrival.

For notice between seven days and two weeks, Article 5(1)(c)(ii) uses two hours
before and less than four hours after.

A replacement that leaves later and arrives earlier meets both timing limbs.
That can defeat fixed compensation on a cancellation theory. It does not create
an Article 5 exemption for involuntary denied boarding.

Article 7(2) is separate. When Article 7 compensation exists and Article 8
re-routing arrives within the applicable two, three, or four-hour limit, the
carrier may reduce the fixed amount by 50%.

## 4. Separate compensation, care, and transport choices

Keep each requested remedy distinct:

- Article 7: fixed compensation.
- Article 8: reimbursement or re-routing choices where referenced.
- Article 9: meals, refreshments, accommodation when necessary, transport
  between airport and accommodation, and two communications.
- Article 10: downgrade reimbursement.

Article 5(3) can remove Article 7 compensation for a proved extraordinary
circumstance that could not have been avoided despite all reasonable measures.
It does not remove cancellation care under Articles 5(1)(b) and 9. McDonagh
confirms that distinction.

## 5. File a precise direct claim

Send one factual package to the carrier:

1. identify the operating carrier and booked journey;
2. state the disruption classification;
3. give original and actual or replacement times with UTC offsets;
4. cite the applicable article and explain each timing limb;
5. separate fixed compensation from itemised care expenses;
6. attach only relevant evidence; and
7. preserve proof of submission.

If the carrier rejects the claim, quote its reason exactly and answer it in the
existing email or portal thread. Preserving the thread maintains the audit trail
and reduces routing errors.

## 6. Escalate after checking competence

Before escalating, check the body’s current rules:

- the National Enforcement Body for the place of incident;
- a competent certified ADR body;
- ECC-Net for eligible cross-border consumer assistance; or
- the court or European Small Claims Procedure where available and appropriate.

An enforcement body may supervise compliance without ordering individual
payment. An ADR body may have separate eligibility and exclusivity rules.
ECC-Net assists with eligible cross-border disputes but is not a court. Never
assume that simultaneous filings are allowed.

## 7. Track the record

Keep a private local ledger containing:

- channel and submission date;
- exact document sent;
- delivery confirmation;
- response date and exact reason;
- forum-specific next date; and
- limitation-period source and calculation.

Do not place passenger records in a public repository.
