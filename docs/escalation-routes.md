# Escalation routes

> **Not legal advice.** Confirm territorial competence, admissibility,
> limitation, exclusivity, cost, and whether the body can order individual
> payment before filing.

## 1. Carrier complaint

Most escalation routes require a prior written claim to the operating carrier.
Use the carrier’s official complaint channel, keep proof of submission, and wait
the period required by the next forum.

If responding to a rejection by email, reply in the existing thread. Preserve
the subject, recipients, prior messages, and `In-Reply-To` metadata.

## 2. National Enforcement Body

Member States designate bodies to enforce Regulation 261/2004. The competent
body is generally connected to where the incident took place, not simply the
carrier’s headquarters or the passenger’s residence.

NEB powers differ. Some investigate and sanction without adjudicating an
individual payment claim; others also operate or connect to a dispute-
resolution process.

Use the current
[European Commission directory](https://transport.ec.europa.eu/transport-themes/passenger-rights/national-enforcement-bodies-neb_en).
The package’s `flightclaim.regulators` module intentionally contains only a
selected set of official links and includes a verify-before-filing warning.

## 3. ECC-Net

[ECC-Net](https://www.eccnet.eu/ecc-network/our-services) offers free
information, advice, and complaint assistance for eligible cross-border
consumer disputes. It generally assists consumers resident in an EU Member
State, Norway, or Iceland against a trader in another participating country.

ECC-Net is not a court and cannot guarantee settlement. Contact the centre for
the consumer’s country of residence and verify residence and cross-border
eligibility.

## 4. Alternative dispute resolution

Check whether a certified ADR body covers:

- the operating carrier;
- the route or place of incident;
- the type and date of disruption;
- the amount and remedy requested; and
- the time elapsed since the carrier complaint.

Read whether the outcome binds either party and whether filing blocks another
ADR or court route.

The European Commission maintains
[ADR guidance and national-body information](https://commission.europa.eu/topics/consumers/consumer-rights-and-complaints/resolve-your-consumer-complaint/alternative-dispute-resolution-consumers_en).
The former EU Online Dispute Resolution platform was discontinued, so do not
rely on old ODR filing links. Because directories and schemes change, verify the
current official service before submitting personal documents.

## 5. Court

Court routes can include a national small-claims procedure or, for qualifying
cross-border civil claims, the European Small Claims Procedure. Jurisdiction,
service, fees, recoverable costs, language, and limitation are national-law
questions outside this tool.

Do not start court proceedings based only on a generic deadline table. Confirm
the forum and calculation from an official source or qualified adviser.

## Route-selection checklist

1. Identify the operating carrier and place of incident.
2. Confirm the direct-claim prerequisite and response period.
3. Read the candidate body’s remedy and binding-effect rules.
4. Check whether another pending proceeding makes the claim inadmissible.
5. Calculate all deadlines from primary or official sources.
6. File one complete, internally consistent evidence package.
